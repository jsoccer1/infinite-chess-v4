    How to Play

    Watch the video walkthrough (opens in a new tab)

    Infinite Chess is chess on a figure-8 board of 72 cells. Each of the four lanes (Q, B, N, R) is a loop that never ends: rooks, bishops and queens keep sliding around the board until something blocks them.

    • White starts in the bottom loop and Black in the top loop (left and right in the horizontal layout). White moves first.
    • The 16 squares in the middle are where the two loops cross. Lanes pass through each other there.
    • Pieces move like normal chess pieces along the board’s lanes and diagonals. Knights jump two steps and one sideways.
    • Bishops, queens and kings can cut diagonally across the narrow tip of each center hole (R7 to R1, and Q12 to Q18). Pawns and knights cannot.
    • Once a pawn enters the 16-square center diamond it can never step back into the loop it came from.
    • Pawns move toward the other loop and promote when White reaches a cell numbered 15 or Black reaches a cell numbered 4.
    • Checkmate wins. Stalemate and threefold repetition are draws.
    • Turn on “Show cell names” in a game to see every cell’s name.

    The bots and their ratings

    Every player starts at 1200. The bots have fixed ratings: Easy 800 (looks one move ahead), Medium 1100 (two moves) and Hard 1400 (three moves).

    Beat a bot in a rated game and you gain points based on the gap between your rating and the bot’s: a stronger bot gives more. Losses and draws against bots never lower your rating, and games shorter than 20 moves are unrated.

    The Dynamic bot

    Dynamic searches as deep as Hard, then adds a memory. Every finished game on the site, against bots or online between players, is added to a shared playbook: for each position, which moves were played and how those games ended. Dynamic plays the moves with a proven winning record and avoids the moves that keep losing. The more games people play, the more positions it knows.

    Unlike the other bots, Dynamic’s rating moves. It started at 1400. After every rated game against it, its rating changes like a player’s (Elo, 24 points at stake): it goes up when it beats someone, more for beating a higher-rated player, and down when it loses. As it learns and wins more, its rating climbs. Choose Dynamic in game settings to see its current rating, how many games it has learned from, and its record.

    Puzzles

    Each puzzle is a position from a real Infinite Chess game with one clear goal: checkmate in 1, checkmate in 2, or win material. Make the moves for the side shown; the opponent replies with its best defence.

    You have a separate puzzle rating that starts at 1000. Solving a puzzle raises it and missing one lowers it, more or less depending on the puzzle’s own rating. Puzzles are picked near your rating, so they get harder as you improve, and each puzzle’s rating also adjusts as players solve or miss it. Choose Easiest to Hardest to shift the range. Using a hint, or trying a puzzle again, does not raise your rating.
